# Terminology Discipline

## Scope

Use this protocol for naming or renaming technical constructs, model objects, mechanisms, regimes, policies, metrics, algorithms, acronyms, or results; resolving conflicting or unsupported usage; or auditing terminology. Ordinary prose and routine reuse of a defined term do not require a decision record.

## Evidence Hierarchy

1. **Current manuscript.** Preserve the formally defined term and its meaning. Record its location in terminology reviews; do not require separate approval for a term already defined by the author.
2. **Identifiable literature.** If the manuscript has no adequate term, use a supplied or actually verified source supporting the meaning. Identify the source by citation key, author-year, title, or location.
3. **Neutral description.** If neither supplies an adequate term, describe the concept without assigning a technical name. Unchecked literature support is unverified, not evidence that no established term exists.

Model familiarity is not provenance. When manuscript and literature terms differ, preserve the manuscript term and mention the literature synonym only when useful for positioning or clarity. A substantive rename is an author decision, not a stylistic substitution.

## Check the Affected Concept

Search available definitions and relevant uses for the concept and its variants. A local edit needs its affected dependencies; a manuscript-wide terminology audit also covers figures, tables, captions, and appendix. State coverage limits when materials are incomplete.

Preserve supported terms consistently. Distinguish source terminology from descriptive paraphrase when reading papers. Do not introduce an unsupported name in an abstract, introduction, proof, or numerical section. Record conflicts and evidence where they affect the requested work, without a ledger for every routine term.

## New-Term Decision

When a new label materially helps, prepare a separate decision note using [the terminology decision template](../assets/terminology-decision-template.md), adapted to the task. Include:

- A neutral concept description and precise candidate definition.
- Manuscript usage and literature evidence actually checked, including gaps.
- Why existing terms are insufficient, alternatives, and ambiguity or collision risks.
- The proposed label, current neutral wording, and author decision status.

Explicitly requesting candidate names authorizes proposals, not automatic adoption. Keep pending candidates in the decision note and neutral wording in other discussion and manuscript prose. Ask for approval only for adoption or a substantive rename; continue all independent drafting and analysis.

Honor an explicit author choice already present in the session. After approval, define the term before substantive use, record the decision and definition location, and reconcile affected manuscript occurrences within the authorized edit scope. Approval of a label does not establish literature usage or prove the underlying claim.

## Reporting and Reuse

For an audit, report the concept, manuscript term and definition, literature evidence if needed, conflicting variants, current wording, and unresolved author decision. Keep approved project terms project-specific in skill maintenance unless independent literature evidence supports broader usage.
